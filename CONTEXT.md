# CONTEXT — glossary

_Dr. Mārcis Gasūns_

This file is a glossary and nothing else. It names the terms used in the CLLG strategy comparison and its derived public page. Implementation details, measurements and decisions belong in the dated documents that cite these terms.

## Structural peer

A project comparable to another at the level of programme shape — kind of corpus, pipeline from source to structured data, openness of distribution, and publication practice — without implying comparability of scale, language, or institutional form. CLLG is a **structural peer** of the open-Sanskrit estate; TLG is a proprietary incumbent against which both define their openness, not a structural peer in the pipeline sense.

## Open classical corpus

A corpus of a classical language whose source transcriptions and derived encodings are distributed under free licences through public repositories, with a citable pipeline from scanned or digitised source to structured data. The term does not require TEI specifically; it requires that the encoding and the route to it be inspectable.

## Closest-analogue mapping

The deliberate act of comparing a concept on one side to the nearest enabling artefact on the other when no true counterpart exists. In the 18-11-2026 scorecard, CLLG's community enablement (released models, datasets, scanner demo) is mapped to the estate's pedagogy/product axis. A closest-analogue mapping is interpretive and is always labelled as such; it is never a like-for-like measurement.

## Verdict scale

The fixed four-value vocabulary used in the scorecard: **LEADS**, **PARITY**, **TRAILS**, **NO COMPARABLE DATA**. The last value is a substantive result for proprietary or structurally different comparators, never a placeholder for unresearched cells.

## Adoption move

A recommendation derived from a scorecard deficit, expressed as an executable unit of work. An adoption move is not a decision: it becomes actionable only when a human selects it and a handoff is minted for it.

## Snapshot

A dated, frozen view of an external source used in a comparison. A snapshot is superseded by a new dated document, never silently updated in place.
