# Devanagari token penalty on our corpora (H6049)

_Generated by [`scripts/token_penalty_h6049.py`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-observatory/blob/main/scripts/token_penalty_h6049.py) + [`scripts/token_penalty_report_h6049.py`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-observatory/blob/main/scripts/token_penalty_report_h6049.py) — deterministic, fixed seeds (bootstrap B=2000, permutation B=10000). Replication of the methodology of arXiv:2609.12960 on estate data._

**Question.** What does it cost, in subword tokens, to write Sanskrit in Devanagari instead of a romanization — on *our* corpora, under the tokenizers our workflows actually ride on? The source paper asks the question per proposition against English; this replication fixes the English question aside and measures the **script axis alone**: Devanagari vs IAST vs Harvard–Kyoto vs SLP1, on identical unit text, penalty relative to SLP1 (the paper's reference script and the native script of CDSL sources).

Known impurity: 0.3% of characters inside MW `<s>` spans are non-SLP1 Unicode (mostly IAST leakage like `Viṣṇu`), plus script-invariant symbols (`˚`, `√`, `—`, ≈6%) that pass through all schemes identically.

## Findings

1. **The penalty is a property of the tokenizer, not of the script.** Devanagari costs 1.07–1.08× under GPT o200k (Hindi-trained merges) but **1.97–2.18× under GPT cl100k and GLM-4** — a ~2× spread across deployed tokenizers on identical text.
2. **GLM-4 is the worst Devanagari tokenizer we ride on.** At 151,329 pieces its Devanagari penalty (1.99 MW / 2.18 DCS) matches cl100k — Devanagari material routed through GLM-family contexts pays roughly double. (Tokenizer: `zai-org/glm-4-9b-chat-hf`.)
3. **o200k inverts the usual story: IAST is its worst scheme** (1.12/1.11), *worse than Devanagari* (1.08/1.07) — diacritic-heavy Latin falls between its merged Hindi graphemes. LLaMA-3 (`unsloth/llama-3.2-3b-instruct`) sits between (Dev 1.20, IAST 1.24–1.32).
4. **HK is the only near-free script everywhere** (1.02–1.04 under every BPE and byte-level). For Latin-routing pipelines HK, not IAST, is the safe pivot.
5. **ByT5 penalty = pure byte-length ratio**: 2.41 (MW) / 2.64 (DCS) ≈ the 3-UTF-8-bytes-per-aksara cost vs 1 byte per SLP1 character; it needs no merges to be wasteful.

## TransLIST-style arm (transliterate-then-tokenize)

The TransLIST approach = transliterate upstream of the tokenizer. With Devanagari→IAST as the upstream step, the arm's penalty collapses to the IAST column; the table below reports what that intervention buys (or costs) per tokenizer:

## Limitations

- Penalty measured on the script-bearing Sanskrit layer (MW) / sentence text (DCS); English glosses excluded by construction, so absolute tokens/unit are not whole-prompt costs.
- MW SLP1 source carries ~0.3% IAST impurity inside `<s>` spans (proper nouns); symbols `˚ √ —` pass through all schemes.
- DCS prose = 5 texts (kathā/satire register); verse and śrauta material may behave differently.
- ByT5 arm = UTF-8 byte counts (byte-level tokenizer, no merges); TransLIST arm is analytic (transliteration is token-free), not a measured transliteration service.
- GLM/LLaMA tokenizers are public HF mirrors of the deployed tokenizers, not vendor-hosted endpoints.
