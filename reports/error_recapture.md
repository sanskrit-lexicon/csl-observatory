# Capture-recapture: how much correction work is left?

_Generated by `scripts/error_recapture.py` from `observatory/site/src/data/correction_events_final.csv` (offline, reproducible). Roadmap: Workstream G3. Estimand: dictionary records ("sites") harbouring at least one error, per dictionary — an order-of-magnitude estimate, not a precision one._

## Design

OBS-T's two correction layers act as two capture occasions over error-prone sites (dict + headword): the **form era** (cfr.tsv web submissions, 2014-2019) and the **git era** (csl-orig commits, 2019-2026). Sites corrected in both eras are "recaptures"; the Chapman estimator converts capture/recapture counts into an estimate of the total error-site population N. Point estimates only where recaptures m >= 10.

**Site key.** Sites are matched on the headword after a scheme repair: NFC + casefold + edge punctuation, with pure-ASCII cells decoded as Harvard-Kyoto, which is what the cfr form used for roman cells. Six candidate joins (including diacritic folding, edit distance 1, and the `<L>` record number) were scored for false matches against csl-orig itself; this is the only one that adds recaptures without linking a single non-identical pair. See `reports/error_recapture_linkage.md` — the looser joins are rejected there with measured rates, not with an opinion.

**Assumption violations, stated plainly.** (1) The occasions are sequential: a site fixed completely in era 1 cannot be recaptured in era 2, which depresses m and biases N upward. (2) Catchability is heterogeneous and positively correlated across eras (both target long, high-traffic entries), biasing N downward. The violations pull in opposite directions; confidence intervals are statistical-only and do not cover these design biases. (3) Population closure is imperfect (new errors can be introduced between eras), a second upward pressure. Read every figure as order-of-magnitude.

## Sensitivity

**(a) Site key with the error component.** Including `error_component` in the site key collapses recaptures (e.g. mw m 107 -> 42, pw m 172 -> 12) — but this reflects missing data, not substance: 77% of form-era events are component-unattributed (`evidence_level = inferred`), so component keys mismatch mechanically. The headword-level key is the honest unit.

**(b) Campaign exclusion.** Dropping the 11,101 events that fall on documented campaign days for their dictionary (obs_t_campaigns.csv) moves the estimable dictionaries to:

| Dict | N (all events) | N (campaigns excluded) |
|---|---:|---:|
| pw | ~76,679 | ~76,094 |
| mw | ~64,284 | ~56,646 |
| bur | ~19,776 | ~4,079 |

**(c) Heterogeneous catchability.** Chapman assumes every site is equally catchable; in reality correctors specialise, so the two eras oversample the same prominent entries and Chapman UNDERESTIMATES. The Chao column is the standard estimate under that heterogeneity scenario (Chao 1987): read Chapman and Chao as the two ends of the plausible range, not as point-and-floor. Both are capped at the record count; for pw and mw the heterogeneity scenario saturates near the whole dictionary — consistent with OCR-derived text where most long entries harbour at least one defect.

**(d) Site key / record linkage.** The join between the two eras was previously the raw headword string. It is now the scheme-repaired key described under Design, which moves the estimable dictionaries to:

| Dict | m (raw key) | m (adopted key) | N (raw key) | N (adopted key) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| pw | 169 | 172 | ~78,194 | ~76,679 |
| mw | 105 | 107 | ~65,497 | ~64,284 |
| bur | 23 | 26 | ~19,776 | ~19,776 |

Looser joins were tested and **rejected on measured evidence**, not left untried — diacritic folding, edit distance 1, and the `<L>` record number each raise m substantially and each manufactures false recaptures, which would shrink the "work remaining" headline by artefact. Because N is inversely proportional to m, a permissive join is not a neutral choice:

## Corrector-pair recapture (within-era cross-check)

The two-era design has one violation it cannot escape: the occasions are five years apart, so a site fixed completely in era 1 cannot be recaptured in era 2, which depresses m and inflates N. Treating two **correctors working the same dictionary in the same era** as the two occasions removes that violation — they sample concurrently — at the cost of a different one: correctors specialise, so their catchability is heterogeneous and positively correlated, which biases the estimate DOWN. A corrector-pair estimate below the two-era estimate for the same dictionary is therefore the expected pattern, and the size of the gap is informative about how much of the two-era figure is the sequential-occasions artefact.

- **A dictionary the two-era design cannot reach but this one can: pwg.** Its two-era overlap is below threshold, yet a single era holds two correctors with enough shared sites to estimate from — so the "not estimable" verdict on a dictionary is a property of the two-era design, not always of the evidence.
- **The spread across corrector pairs is the heterogeneity, measured.** In pw (form era) the estimate ranges ~9,849 to ~57,370 depending on which two correctors are treated as the occasions — a factor of 5.8. Sensitivity (c) argues from theory that catchability is heterogeneous; this is that heterogeneity as a number, and it bounds how much precision any single Chapman figure in this report can carry.
- Joint attributions (`a/b`, `_etal`) and handle pairs one keystroke apart are excluded: neither is two independent observers. What remains still assumes a corrector's handle is one person and that two correctors chose their entries independently — the second assumption is certainly false in a coordinated campaign, which is why this is a cross-check on the order of magnitude rather than a replacement estimate.

## Reading

- The two eras overlap remarkably little: of 40,190 observed error sites org-wide, only a few hundred were touched in both eras. Under mark-recapture logic, low overlap between two substantial samples means the underlying population is LARGE: what has been corrected so far is a minority of what exists.
- **pw**: ~76,679 error-prone records estimated = ~45% of its 170,556 records; 10,879 corrected so far = **~14% of the estimated work done**, ~65,800 records still awaiting a first correction (Chapman scenario; under heterogeneity the remaining share is larger).
- **mw**: ~64,284 error-prone records estimated = ~22% of its 286,525 records; 6,716 corrected so far = **~10% of the estimated work done**, ~57,568 records still awaiting a first correction (Chapman scenario; under heterogeneity the remaining share is larger).
- **bur**: ~19,776 error-prone records estimated (estimate capped at the full dictionary) = ~100% of its 19,776 records; 1,538 corrected so far = **~8% of the estimated work done**, ~18,238 records still awaiting a first correction (Chapman scenario; under heterogeneity the remaining share is larger).
- Most dictionaries cannot be estimated yet — their two-era overlap is below threshold. That is itself a finding: correction effort has been so concentrated (and era-partitioned) that for most dictionaries we cannot even bound the remaining work from correction history alone.
